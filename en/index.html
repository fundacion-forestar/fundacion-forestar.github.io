Our Journey

Forestar’s progress

From our first planting to our current work at Caño Juriepe: the concrete steps we took to build the foundation and move the project toward certification.

April 2019

First planting: 2 hectares

Six months after the organization was founded, we planted the first 2 hectares of restoration — the starting point of Forestar’s field work.

September 2019

Arauca Environmental Development Roundtable

Five months after our first planting, we joined the Environmental Development Roundtable of the Arauca department, our first institutional coordination space.

2021

Beekeeping Sector Strengthening Project

We took part in the departmental project to strengthen the beekeeping sector. Since then, we have combined reforestation with ecosystem management and improvement through beekeeping.

2023

Departmental Agro-industrial Science and Technology Roundtable

We joined the departmental agro-industrial science and technology roundtable of Arauca.

May 2026

Land-use agreement signed

We formalized the 150-hectare land-use agreement in Vereda La Esperanza, Cravo Norte, expanding the restoration area of the Caño Juriepe project.

May 2026

Legal registrations updated

We renewed the tax ID (RUT) and the Arauca Chamber of Commerce Certificate, and completed the PADOR registration with the European Union to qualify the foundation as an eligible partner in European calls for proposals.

June 2026

Proposal submitted to PPD Colombia (GEF/UNDP)

We submitted the Caño Juriepe project proposal to the Global Environment Facility Small Grants Programme, in coordination with UNDP Colombia.

June 2026

Coordination with CORPORINOQUIA

The regional environmental authority requested the project executive summary as part of the institutional support process.

September 2026

New proposals to the Arauca Secretariat for Agricultural and Sustainable Development

We submitted three proposals for food security and water access in seven municipalities of the Arauca department.

Frequently Asked Questions

All about carbon credits

Gold Standard is an international carbon certification standard founded in 2003 by WWF and other environmental organizations. It requires every tonne of CO₂ to be verified by independent third parties and the project to deliver real benefits for local communities and biodiversity.

We use IPCC methodology to measure forest biomass in the field. Before any credit is issued, an independent auditor accredited by Gold Standard must verify the calculations. Our current estimate is 31.5 tCO₂/ha.

Once your reservation is confirmed, we immediately send you confirmation with your package details. The official Gold Standard certificate — including serial number, GPS coordinates of the area, and issue date — is issued once the project completes its registration and verification process with Gold Standard. We keep you informed of progress at every stage.

We are a foundation from the Llanos working in Cravo Norte since 2018. The project combines 12 native Orinoco species, meliponiculture with stingless native bees, and a team of professionals from the region. We have submitted it to EUROCLIMA+ (European Union) and the GEF/UNDP Small Grants Programme.

Gold Standard credits serve voluntary goals: ESG reporting, beyond value chain mitigation (SBTi BVCM) and neutralization of residual emissions in net-zero strategies. They do not replace reducing your own emissions, and in the European Union they cannot be used to market a product as "carbon neutral" (Directive 2024/825). For mandatory compensation before Colombian authorities, contact us and we will advise you on the applicable mechanisms.
